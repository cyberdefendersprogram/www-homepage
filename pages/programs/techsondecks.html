What is AI Village?

AI Village is a hands-on corner of the event where you learn how AI can be fooled, by trying it yourself. Chatbots and AI assistants are showing up everywhere: in help desks, apps, email and school tools. Companies that build them need people who know how they can go wrong, and testing an AI by trying to break it is a real job called AI red teaming. At our table you try a safe, beginner version of it. Nothing you do can hurt anything. It's a game.

How it works

Skipper is the ship's AI. It knows a secret launch code and has been told to protect it. Your job is to get it to slip. Talk to it, be creative, and type the code in (or watch it happen) to clear each level. Fewer messages means a higher score, and the best cadets show up on the live leaderboard at our table.

  1. Pick a callsign. A nickname, not your real name.
  2. Clear the four levels. Each one teaches something new (see below).
  3. Stuck? Use a hint. Hints unlock as you go, and cost a few points.
  4. Save your badge. Download a Techs on Decks badge with your callsign and score.

What each level teaches

  • Level 1, Deck Hand: AI believes what you tell it. A chatbot has no way to check who you are. Sometimes a good reason is all it needs.
  • Level 2, Officer of the Deck: pressure works on AI too. Urgency, authority and friendly requests can push an AI off its rules.
  • Level 3, Radio Room: AI agents. Now Skipper can do things: read a logbook and send radio messages. An AI that uses tools can be talked into using them the wrong way, even when it refuses to say a secret in chat.
  • Level 4, Vault Door: attacks that take several steps. Skipper has to look up a code and then use it. Real AI attacks often chain steps like this.

The big idea has a name: prompt injection. It is the number one risk on the OWASP Top 10 for LLM applications. It happens when an AI follows instructions from the wrong person, because it can't always tell instructions from information. You don't need to code to try it. You just need words.

Why it matters

  • Think before you trust AI. It can be wrong, and it can be tricked, just like a person can.
  • Never put passwords or private info into a chatbot. You don't know where it goes or who else can get it out.
  • There are real careers here. Security analysts, penetration testers and AI red teamers all start by learning how things break.

Like this? Come learn cybersecurity at Merritt College

If trying to outsmart an AI was fun, you'd probably enjoy the real thing. Merritt College teaches cybersecurity hands-on, and we'd love to see you in a class. Here is what the program offers:

  • A two-year degree. Earn an Associate of Science in Infrastructure Security or Applications Security, with flexible Friday classes and real-world experience through internships and labs.
  • Two tracks. Both start with computer science and security basics, then move on to topics like ethical hacking, secure coding, network defense and cloud security.
  • Real classes with real names. Hacker Techniques, Exploits, and Incident Handling. Cloud Security Fundamentals. Web Application PEN Testing. Computer Forensics Fundamentals. There's also a short intro, Cyber Safety, Online Identity, and Computer Literacy.
  • CyberCamp. A free, two-week intro to cybersecurity for middle schoolers, high schoolers and adults, taught by college faculty.
  • Competitions and careers. Merritt students compete in the National Cyber League and attend the annual Cybersecurity Career Day.
